# Independent Design Directions

Status: provisional selection pending moderated usability evidence

These directions were developed from v2 journeys without legacy interface, screenshots, assets, hierarchy, copy, or style reference.

## Direction A: Field Notebook

Visual language: editorial research notebook, strong numbered steps, warm paper surface, ink-like type, lane-specific annotation colors, and evidence presented as field notes.

Interaction: sequential seed, intent, and results sections remain visible in one document. Advanced controls open inside intent section. Result explanations unfold inline.

Strengths: reinforces inspectability and deliberate discovery; avoids dashboard metaphors; supports long-form evidence.

Risks: dense result cards may feel slow on narrow screens; metaphor must not reduce contrast or suggest uncertain data is factual observation.

## Direction B: Signal Console

Visual language: dark technical instrument, high-contrast waveforms, compact lane channels, restrained phosphor accents, and explicit degradation indicators.

Interaction: intent controls stay in fixed signal strip while ranked output updates beside it. Evidence opens as trace from score factor to source excerpt.

Strengths: makes cause and effect visible; efficient for repeated refinement; strong retrieval-state communication.

Risks: can expose system vocabulary too early; may resemble operational tooling rather than player product.

## Direction C: Curated Shelf

Visual language: generous gallery, image-led editorial cards, typographic recommendations, muted material colors, and small evidence labels.

Interaction: short guided intent interview leads to a curated shelf. Detailed weights and exclusions live in secondary editing sheet.

Strengths: fastest initial comprehension; inviting on mobile; metadata supports Steam decision.

Risks: artwork and familiarity may overpower semantic reasons; advanced refinement becomes less immediate.

## Provisional Choice

Field Notebook is implemented for prototype evaluation because it best supports deterministic explanation and progressive intent without system jargon. Choice is not accepted by familiarity or implementation cost. Moderated mobile and desktop tasks must compare comprehension and completion against low-fidelity Signal Console and Curated Shelf prototypes before launch acceptance.

## Test Tasks

- Select one known game and identify two valued qualities.
- Set one semantic lane to zero and predict expected change.
- Explain why first recommendation appeared without facilitator help.
- Find source evidence for one contribution.
- Exclude one concept and refine results.
- Share intent and reopen it on another viewport.

Record completion, time, errors, control comprehension, explanation accuracy, and preference rationale. No success claim is made until sessions occur.
